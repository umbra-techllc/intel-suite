How the projections work

Usage first. Receiving yards start from target share times projected team pass attempts. Rushing yards start from carry share times projected team carries. Passing yards start from projected attempts times yards per attempt. Share and efficiency are more stable week to week than raw yardage.

Small samples get pulled toward a baseline. Recent games count more, but every rate is blended with the player's 2025 numbers (or a position average) until the 2026 sample is big enough to stand on its own.

Game script from the market. The spread moves volume: favorites run more and throw less, underdogs do the opposite. The implied team total nudges efficiency up or down.

Opponent. Each defense's yards allowed versus league average, by unit and by receiving position, shrunk toward average over its first four games.

Injuries. Players listed Out, Doubtful or on IR are removed. When a team's remaining receivers or backs account for less than a normal share of the work, their shares are scaled up to cover what was vacated.

Hit rates. Outcomes are modeled with a right-skewed distribution centered on the projection, since yardage has a hard floor and a long upper tail. Edge is the model's hit rate minus the break-even rate implied by the odds.

Parlays. Legs from the same game are adjusted for correlation (a QB and his receivers move together, a big-favorite run game pulls against its own passing). Legs from different games are treated as independent.

Umbra Read. A written take on a prop or a parlay, generated from the same numbers on this page: the projection, drivers, line, odds, game context and game log. It doesn't see news beyond the injury report, so treat it as a second opinion on the model, not new information.
