How the projections work

A model trained on eight seasons. Each projection comes from models trained on about 24,000 player-games from 2019 to this season, using only what was known before each game. They weigh about 45 inputs: recent games, how often a player gets the ball and what he does with it, last season, his quarterback, his team's play calling, expected scoring, the opponent, injured teammates, and a basic estimate from his share of plays (described below). Tested on seasons they never saw, they were about 4 to 6% more accurate than that basic estimate alone. "Biggest factors" shows how each projection was reached, in yards.

Each player's own range. For runs and catches, a second model predicts how widely each player's results swing, so a deep-threat receiver gets a wider range than a short-pass target. For passing, ranges come from how quarterbacks with similar projections actually finished.

Opportunities first. Receiving yards start from how often passes go to a player times how many passes his team throws. Rushing yards start from his share of the team's runs. Passing yards start from expected passes times yards per pass. How often a player gets the ball is steadier week to week than how many yards he gains.

Small samples get pulled toward a baseline. Recent games count more, but every rate is blended with last season's numbers (or a position average) until this season's sample can stand on its own.

Opponent and expected scoring. Each defense's yards allowed against league average, by type of play and position, plus how many points the team is expected to score. Teams favored to win run a bit more.

Injuries and backups. Players ruled out, doubtful or on injured reserve are removed. Their share of the work moves to teammates by rules measured on every 2024-25 game where a regular sat out: running backs pick up a real share of a missing back's runs, while receivers gain less than you'd expect because the offense as a whole tends to get worse. Starting quarterbacks come from the team's depth chart, so a backup who hasn't played yet still gets a projection.

Chances are measured, not assumed. Outcome ranges are fit on predictions made for seasons the models were not trained on, so the chances reflect real errors. On 2024 and 2025, the over/under calls scored better than the previous version in every market.

The sportsbook gets half the say. Sportsbook lines are sharp, so the chance shown is half the projection's view and half the book's (with the book's cut removed). A pick needs at least a 2-point edge over break-even (Lean) or 4 points (Play). If the two disagree by more than 25 points, the prop is marked "Check news" instead, since that usually means something the projection hasn't seen.

Most likely mode. Shows what each player will most likely do and the bet most likely to hit. The player's own range is shifted so that its chance at the sportsbook's main line matches the blended chance there, and every other line is read off that same range, so alternate lines get the book's view too. The likely bet is the best-paying line with at least a 75% chance (skipping anything priced shorter than −600, which pays too little to matter). "Fair price" is the price that would exactly match the chance; "good price" means the book pays at least that.

Parlay chances. The chance that every leg hits is simulated 60,000 times with all the legs' measured links at once (a Gaussian copula), so it stays accurate for any number of legs. The most likely parlay picks the legs with the highest chances that don't work against each other, with at most two per game unless they help each other.

When there's no pick. No sportsbook line, fewer than two games this season, or a small role.

Parlays. Legs from the same game are adjusted by how they actually moved together in 2024-25: a quarterback and his own receivers tend to hit together, two opposing quarterbacks a little, and most other pairs barely affect each other. The builder picks by rule from the strongest edges and never pairs legs that work against each other.

Umbra Read. A plain-English explanation of the pick the numbers already made, including who is out and how that changes things. It only uses the data on this page and the injury report, so it explains the projection rather than adding news.
