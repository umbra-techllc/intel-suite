How the predictions work

Trained on eight seasons. Each chance comes from a model trained on every player-game since 2019, using only what was known before kickoff. Tested on the 2024 and 2025 seasons, which it never trained on, it beat a player's own touchdown rate on every measure and ranked scorers noticeably better.

Getting the ball near the end zone matters most. About 85% of rushing touchdowns and 71% of receiving touchdowns start inside the 20-yard line. The model looks at how often each player gets the ball inside the 20, the 10 and the 5, passes thrown to him in the end zone, and his share of his team's chances there.

Team and game. How many points the betting market expects his team to score, how often the team has been scoring touchdowns, whether it tends to run or pass near the end zone, and how many touchdowns the opponent has allowed to his position.

The quarterback. Every receiver's chance includes his starting quarterback's touchdown-pass rate, his rate near the end zone, and his last three games. A backup starting changes those numbers.

Injuries. Players ruled out are removed, and the model accounts for teammates who are missing and the end-zone chances they leave behind. Starting quarterbacks come from each team's depth chart.

The chances are honest. Checked against what happened in seasons the model did not train on, players it gave a 25% chance scored about 25% of the time, and the same held at every level.

QB touchdown passes. A second model predicts each starting quarterback's expected touchdown passes from his own trends, his team's expected scoring and the opponent. The chances of 1+, 2+ and 3+ come from that expectation.

Parlays. Touchdown scorers barely move together, with one exception: when a quarterback throws two or more touchdowns, his own receivers and tight ends are clearly more likely to score. The parlay chance accounts for that, simulated 60,000 times.
